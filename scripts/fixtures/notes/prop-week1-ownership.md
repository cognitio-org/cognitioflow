## Possession and detention

Art 3:107 BW distinguishes **possession** (holding for oneself) from **detention** (holding for
another). The distinction decides acquisitive prescription and the Art 3:86 protection.
[LECTURE]

| Concept | Held for | Example | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Possession | Oneself | A buyer who has taken delivery | Prescription runs; Art 3:86 available |
| Detention | Another | A tenant, a carrier, a repairer | Neither |

- [!] A detentor cannot convert into a possessor by a private change of mind — *interversie* needs
  an external act. The slide's phrasing suggests otherwise. [WG] [SLIDES]

## Bottom line

Ownership is comprehensive and limited only by law; property rights are a closed list; possession
is holding for oneself and it is the gateway to prescription and to good-faith protection.
