# Week 3 — Security rights: pledge and mortgage

*Fixture content. Not real coursework.*

●● Mortgage (*hypotheek*) attaches to registered property; pledge (*pand*) to everything else.
Art 3:227 BW. Both are accessory to the secured claim: the claim dies, the security dies with it.
[LECTURE]

- ● Possessory pledge (Art 3:236) needs the thing out of the pledgor's hands. Non-possessory
  pledge (Art 3:237) uses a notarial or registered private deed and leaves the pledgor in
  possession — which is why it is the commercial default. [LECTURE]
- ● Priority is by time of creation, subject to registration. A later registered mortgage beats an
  earlier unregistered one. [READER]
- ○ The *fiducia cum creditore* history explains why the non-possessory pledge looks the way it
  does. Background. [READER]

| Right | Object | Creation | Enforcement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mortgage | Registered property | Notarial deed + registration | Public sale, Art 3:268 |
| Possessory pledge | Movables | Delivery into the creditor's control | Public sale, Art 3:250 |
| Non-possessory pledge | Movables, claims | Notarial or registered deed | Same, after notice |

> **The trap.** Enforcement is by **public** sale unless the court permits otherwise. An agreement
> made in advance that the creditor may simply keep the thing is void — Art 3:235, the prohibition
> on *toe-eigening*. It is void even if both parties are commercial and the price is fair. [WG]

- [!] correction: the prohibition bites on agreements made **before** default. A post-default
  agreement to hand the thing over in satisfaction is a different animal. The handout conflates
  them. [WG]

## Bottom line

Accessory, registered or delivered, enforced by public sale, and never by a pre-agreed forfeiture.
