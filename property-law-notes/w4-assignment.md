# Week 4 — Transfer of Claims (Assignment)

*van der Linden, "Assignment" · Salomons, "Deformalisation" (syllabus; not in the project files) · DCFR Book III Ch 5 s 1 · Engine E6*

> Gate (E6): a claim is the active side of an obligation but also an asset belonging to the creditor's property, so its transfer sits "at the crossroad" of obligations and property law. Assignment is a three-party phenomenon (assignor, assignee, debtor). Distinguish the assignment itself from the debtor's position, and the proprietary from the obligatory effect of every rule.

## Effects

- **New creditor — III.-5:113**: assignor ceases, assignee becomes creditor.

- **What passes — III.-5:115**: primary right plus accessory rights and transferable supporting securities.

- **Nemo plus**: the assignee gets the same claim, subject to prior limited rights (droit de suite); the debtor keeps **defences** and **set-off** — **III.-5:116** (exceptions: debtor induced belief there were none; defence based on breach of an anti-assignment clause; set-off for cross-claims existing before discharge-by-paying-assignor ended, or closely connected).

- **Place of performance — III.-5:117**: money may be demanded anywhere in the same country/EU; assignor bears extra cost.

- **Assignor's undertakings — III.-5:112**: right exists, assignor entitled, no defences/set-off, no prior assignment or encumbrance, no undisclosed modifications; **no warranty of the debtor's solvency** (5:112(7)).

- **Causal consequences — III.-5:118**: void underlying contract ⇒ no assignment; **avoidance is retroactive** (right treated as never passed — (3)); **withdrawal, termination and revocation are not** ((4)) — the claim stays with the assignee until re-assigned (cf. III.-3:510–3:511). This is the **insolvency test** of the causal system.

## Debtor protection

- **III.-5:119(1)** — a debtor who has not received notice and does not know of the assignment is **discharged by paying the assignor**; (2)–(3) good-faith payment to a person named as assignee in a notice from the assignor.

- **III.-5:120** — the debtor may demand a compliant notice (textual form, durable medium, adequate information) or reliable evidence from an assignee who alone gave notice, and may **withhold performance** meanwhile.

- **III.-5:108(2)–(3)** — where an anti-assignment clause is breached the debtor may still pay the assignor and set off as if unassigned, unless the debtor consented, misled the assignee, or the right is payment for goods/services (**trade receivables carve-out**).

## Priority

- **III.-5:121(1)** — successive assignments: the assignee **first to notify** the debtor has priority, provided that assignee neither knew nor ought to have known of the earlier assignment **at the time of the later assignment** (not at notification); **(2)** the debtor is discharged by paying the first to notify even if aware of competing demands.

- **III.-5:122** — assignee's right to identifiable **proceeds** held by the assignor has priority over competing claimants.

- Van der Linden criticises the first-to-notify rule as impractical for bulk non-notification financing.

> Flag (PRECISION): the reading once cites "III.-5:119 paragraph 1" for the double-assignment rule; the correct provision is III.-5:121(1). Rely on the DCFR file.

## Salomons — Deformalisation (syllabus item)

Listed in the syllabus (CSECL Working Paper 2007/01) but **not present in the project files**; the engine relies on the lecture. The thesis to carry: the European trend is away from strict notification formalities toward "silent" assignment, which shifts the risk of not knowing the creditor onto the **debtor** — hence the compensating rules on discharge, proof and set-off above.

## Exam hooks and traps

- "A assigns to B; the factoring contract is avoided / terminated; B insolvent — who owns the claim?" → III.-5:118(3) vs (4); DE contrast.

- "Debtor pays A after A assigned to B — discharged?" → III.-5:119(1); proof rights III.-5:120.

- "A assigns the same claim to B then C; C notifies first" → III.-5:121; good faith tested at the *later assignment*; debtor protection (2).

- "Contract contains a non-assignment clause" → no proprietary effect (5:108(1)); obligatory effects (2); trade-receivables carve-out (3).

- "Can D raise defences/set-off against the new creditor?" → III.-5:116; nemo plus.

- "Why does the DCFR treat assignment as both proprietary and obligatory?" → parallel of III.-5:104 and VIII.-2:101.

- **Traps:** analysing the claim like a physical good; confusing proprietary and obligatory effects of an anti-assignment clause; testing good faith at notification; treating termination as retroactive; forgetting the assignor does not warrant the debtor's solvency.
