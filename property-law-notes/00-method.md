# The Method — How Every Answer Is Built

> This is an applied, technique-driven exam, not a memorisation contest. Raw content recall alone scores roughly a third of the marks; the rest live in answer machinery — defining first, naming the exact doctrine and article, answering the literal demand, solving the case party-by-party, and concluding.
> Two things make this course specific. It is article-anchored: almost every point must be substantiated with a DCFR provision (Book III Ch 5 s1 = assignment; Book VIII = acquisition/loss of ownership of goods; Book IX = proprietary security) or a national provision — never invent or half-remember a number; cite from the reference tables on this page. It is case-solving: working-group questions look like "A sells to B, C registers a mortgage, D lives there — discuss the rights and obligations and against whom they can be exerted." The unit of work is a party-by-party, right-by-right analysis.

## 1. The answer machine

Run this sequence on every question:

1. **Restate the literal demand** in one line at the top ("The question asks: *who owns the car and can the trustee reclaim it?*"). Answer *that* first.

2. **Define the load-bearing concept** in your own words (numerus clausus, tradition, causa, good-faith acquisition…). Definitions-first earns marks and prevents drift.

3. **Name the precise doctrine + article.** "Derivative acquisition governed by VIII.-2:101" beats "the transfer rules."

4. **Characterise before you apply.** Property or personal right? Original or derivative acquisition? Causal or abstract, consensual or tradition? Characterisation is where the marks are won or lost.

5. **Apply, clause-by-clause / party-by-party.** Walk each requirement of the article against the facts; walk each party and state the nature of their right and against whom it runs.

6. **Comparative overlay where invited.** Say how the answer differs across FR / DE / NL / England / DCFR — a recurring source of easy marks.

7. **Conclude.** State the outcome explicitly. Address every sub-question.

## 2. The engines

Each engine has a **Gate** (state before touching facts), **Steps**, a **Trigger**, and **Traps**.

### E1 — Property right vs personal right (+ numerus clausus)

- **Gate:** a property right is *absolute* (erga omnes); a personal right binds only between the parties (inter partes).

- **Steps:** (1) classify the right; (2) give the hallmarks (absolute effect, droit de suite, droit de préférence, publicity); (3) invoke numerus clausus and its *purpose* (third-party transparency, legal certainty); (4) state against *whom* the right can be exerted.

- **Trigger:** "characteristics of property vs personal rights"; any "discuss the rights of A/B/C/D and against whom" case.

- **Traps:** describing a right without saying *against whom* it runs; forgetting to tie numerus clausus to its purpose; confusing *specificity* with numerus clausus.

### E2 — Ownership vs possession

- **Gate:** ownership = the most comprehensive *right* (VIII.-1:202); possession = *factual* control with intention (VIII.-1:205–1:206), a fact protected by law.

- **Steps:** (1) define each; (2) note possession can ground later ownership via acquisitive prescription (E9); (3) flag the common-law contrast (relative title, estates) where asked.

- **Traps:** treating possession as a weaker kind of ownership; missing the civil-law/common-law contrast when asked.

### E3 — Original vs derivative acquisition (production, commingling, combination)

- **Gate:** *original or derivative?* Derivative acquisition comes from a predecessor (**nemo dat** and the power to dispose matter); original acquisition creates a fresh title (nemo dat is irrelevant).

- **Steps:** (1) classify the mode; (2) apply the Book VIII Ch 5 rule — production **VIII.-5:201**, commingling **VIII.-5:202**, combination **VIII.-5:203** — to decide *who owns the result*; (3) resolve the *compensation / security-right* leg for the party who lost ownership (**VIII.-5:204**); (4) check party autonomy (**VIII.-5:101**) — wide in the DCFR, mandatory in NL.

- **Traps:** running nemo dat on what is actually original acquisition; solving *who owns it* but forgetting compensation; mislabelling combination as commingling.

### E4 — Transfer of goods: the requirements machine + system classification

- **Gate:** classify the system on **two independent axes**: causal vs abstract, and consensual vs delivery (tradition).

- **Steps:** (1) run **VIII.-2:101(1)** clause by clause — (a) goods exist; (b) transferable; (c) transferor's right or authority; (d) transferee's entitlement; (e) agreement on time or else delivery/equivalent (**VIII.-2:104–2:105**); (2) locate the missing requirement and make it the hinge; (3) state the system's position — **causal** (FR, NL, DCFR: **VIII.-2:202**) vs **abstract** (DE); **consensual** (FR: art. 1196 CC; SGA ss. 17–18) vs **tradition** (DE § 929, NL art. 3:84, DCFR); (4) conclude on *when and whether* ownership passed.

- **Traps:** collapsing the two axes; asserting a requirement is met without checking the *power to dispose*; forgetting that in a causal system a defective causa kills the transfer.

### E5 — Third-party protection: good-faith acquisition of movables

- **Gate:** start from **nemo dat quod non habet**, then ask whether the good-faith-acquisition *exception* rescues the acquirer.

- **Steps:** (1) confirm the transferor lacked right/authority; (2) run **VIII.-3:101** — other 2:101 requirements met; *delivery or equivalent* (no constitutum possessorium); *for value*; good faith (neither knew nor could reasonably be expected to know — **burden on the acquirer**); stolen goods excluded unless sold in the ordinary course of business; stolen cultural objects never; (3) conclude; (4) name this as the first of Salomons' **two gatekeepers** — pair with E9.

- **Traps:** protecting the acquirer without first establishing the nemo dat failure; ignoring value / possession; missing the stolen-goods limitation; assuming good faith is presumed (it is not, under VIII.-3:101).

### E6 — Transfer of claims (assignment)

- **Gate:** a claim is an *asset*; assignment is its transfer (Book III Ch 5 s1) — distinguish the *assignment* (assignor→assignee) from the *debtor's* position.

- **Steps:** (1) requirements — **III.-5:104**: right exists, assignable, right/authority, entitlement, act of assignment; no notice or consent of the debtor needed (para 4); (2) **debtor protection** — **III.-5:119** (discharge by paying the assignor until notice), **III.-5:120** (proof), **III.-5:116** (defences and set-off survive); (3) **priority** among successive assignments — **III.-5:121** (first to notify, if in good faith *at the time of the later assignment*); (4) causal consequences — **III.-5:118** (avoidance retroactive; termination not); (5) conclude on who can claim from the debtor.

- **Traps:** analysing it like a transfer of a physical good; ignoring notification's role; testing good faith at notification rather than at assignment; treating termination as retroactive.

### E7 — Land law: transfer of land + registration systems

- **Gate:** name the **register type** on three axes before analysing third-party effect: rights vs documents registered; **constitutive vs declaratory**; **good faith in the register protected or not** (positive vs negative).

- **Steps:** (1) classify — DE Grundbuch (constitutive, § 873 BGB, public faith); England (LRA 2002 s. 7, s. 23 — "registration is the right"); FR (declaratory, documents, no good-faith protection, décret 1955 art. 30); NL (constitutive, arts 3:84 jo 3:89 BW, but "negative" — no cure of defects, only partial protection under arts 3:24–3:26, and art. 3:88 does not protect against a predecessor's lack of power); (2) state how land transfers; (3) analyse third-party protection *through the register*; (4) rank limited rights by registration date; priority notices (§ 883 BGB; art. 7:3 BW).

- **Traps:** conflating deeds with title registration; assuming constitutive ⇒ positive (NL breaks the correlation); forgetting whether registration is constitutive or for third-party effect only; the DCFR has **no land law**.

### E8 — Limited rights (characterisation, nemo plus, ranking)

- **Gate:** a limited right is *carved out of* a mother right — **nemo plus iuris**. Numerus clausus applies (**VIII.-1:204** categories).

- **Steps:** (1) identify the limited right (usufruct, servitude, mortgage, pledge); (2) it binds third parties (droit de suite); (3) rank competing limited rights by time of creation (*prior tempore potior iure*) — under Book IX, by time of *effectiveness* (**IX.-4:101**), with superpriority for acquisition finance (**IX.-4:102**); (4) trace the effect of transfer or enforcement of the mother right — a prior mortgage enforced clears a later usufruct.

- **Traps:** treating limited rights as personal; ranking backwards; forgetting that a limited right survives a transfer but yields to enforcement of a *prior* right; assuming a DCFR retention of ownership binds third parties without registration (**IX.-3:107**).

### E9 — Acquisitive prescription (+ Pye v UK)

- **Gate:** prescription reallocates ownership from an inactive owner to a long-term possessor — the second of Salomons' **two gatekeepers**.

- **Steps:** (1) requirements — **VIII.-4:101**: owner-possessor, continuous possession, **10 years** in good faith throughout (good faith presumed) or **30 years** otherwise; the thief never; (2) contrast with good-faith acquisition (E5) — good faith *at acquisition* vs *time/possession*; (3) national periods (NL arts 3:99 / 3:105 / 3:306 BW; DE § 937 BGB); (4) **Pye v UK** [GC] 2007: adverse possession = **control of use** under A1P1, legitimate aim (legal certainty), fair balance, no compensation required → **no violation** (10–7), reversing the 2005 Chamber.

- **Traps:** confusing acquisitive with extinctive prescription; stating Pye backwards; forgetting the DCFR excludes immovables.

### E0 — The party-by-party case machine

1. Fix the timeline of juridical acts (sale, delivery, registration, default).

2. For **each party**: what right (property or personal — E1), how acquired (E3/E4), and **against whom**?

3. Resolve every **conflict** with ranking/priority and third-party-protection engines (E5, E7, E8).

4. Conclude for each party separately. A missed party is a missed mark.

## 3. Error types to hunt

- **GATE_SKIP** — answering adjacent material instead of the literal demand (describing "the transfer rules" instead of saying *who owns it now*). Fix: the one-line literal restatement at the top.

- **WRONG_TOOL** — reaching for the adjacent-but-wrong concept (see the disambiguation pairs). Fix: name the pair and say which side you are on.

- **PRECISION** — right concept, wrong article or label (wrong DCFR number, wrong ranking order, Pye backwards). Fix: verify against the reference tables.

## 4. The diagnosis loop

1. Cold, timed mock on a topic. 2. Mark hard against the rubric above. 3. Extract *named* failure modes ("GATE_SKIP on Q3: classified the system but never applied VIII.-2:101 to the facts"). 4. 30-minute gap-closure block per failure: restate the concept → redo the exact question cold → re-mark. 5. Second cold mock. 6. Consolidate to a one-page cheat sheet; cold-recall self-tests; drill only the survivors. **Highest-value move:** marks-earning rewrites of cold-mock failures, immediately after seeing where they bled.
