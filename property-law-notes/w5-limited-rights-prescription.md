## Part A — Limited rights (Verstijlen; Engine E8)

**Gate.** Ownership is "the most comprehensive right" (**VIII.-1:202**); limited rights are less comprehensive rights *in re aliena*, carved out of ownership, yet still **absolute** property rights good against everyone including the owner (droit de suite). **Nemo plus**: the owner can transfer only encumbered ownership. **Numerus clausus** caps the list; the DCFR has no list of its own (**VIII.-1:204** names the categories — security rights, rights to use, rights to acquire (2:307), trust-related rights) and defers to national law except for security rights (Book IX) and trusts (Book X).

**Conflicting limited rights.** *Prior tempore potior iure*: the earlier usufruct or pledge prevails, though a later right is not worthless (residual years; surplus proceeds); a mortgage and a usufruct only clash on **enforcement**, and enforcement of the senior right clears the junior one.

**Creation mirrors transfer.** NL art. 3:98 BW applies the transfer rules to the creation of limited rights; DE § 1032 (usufruct) and § 1205 (pledge) copy § 929. A possessory pledge is commercially impractical — hence the Dutch non-possessory pledge by registered or authentic deed (art. 3:237 BW — printed as "2:237" in the reading; verify) and the German *Sicherungsübereignung*.

**Book IX — creation vs effectiveness (UCC Art. 9 model).**

| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Scope                               | IX.-1:101; IX.-1:102            | any limited proprietary right giving preferential satisfaction; a security transfer of ownership creates only a security right; retention-of-ownership devices governed by Book IX (IX.-1:103–1:104)                                  |

**Retention of ownership nationally.** Directive 2011/7/EU art. 9(1) requires it to be available; implemented as a conditional transfer — § 449 BGB, art. 3:92 BW, art. 2367 CC (all in the national provisions file). ECJ C-302/05 *Commission v Italy*: the Directive requires effectiveness against the **buyer**, not necessarily against other creditors. Illustrations from the reading: the **printing press** (buyer pledges the machine before the seller registers → 35-day window decides) and the **timber/chairs** case (bank's registered security over stock vs supplier's retention with extension clause → IX.-2:308, 4:102, 4:103).

## Part B — Acquisitive prescription (Hoops; Engine E9)

**Function.** A mode of acquisition based not on agreement plus publicity but on **qualified control over time**; it may heal a failed transfer, settle boundaries, or reward land theft. Justifications: restoring legal certainty by aligning law with physical reality; curing proof problems (strong for movables, weak for registered land except where a *registered* transfer failed); French law adds the owner's duty of care towards society. Alternatives: Radin's personhood theory; law-and-economics efficiency.

**Three recurring requirements.**

1. **Qualified physical control** — holding for oneself / proprietary possession / possessing as owner, inferred from acts that **exclude the owner** (fences, gates, permanent alterations); mere use or paying rent is not enough. Cass. 25498/2014 (IT: grazing 10 cattle insufficient for 300 ha); HR 24 Feb 2017, ECLI:NL:HR:2017:309 (NL: 400 m² fenced municipal woodland — control as owner; and a tort damages claim against the bad-faith "land thief" *after* prescription).

2. **Knowledge (good faith)** — either bars acquisition (DE § 937(2) BGB) or shortens the period (NL, IT, Louisiana); NL art. 3:118(2) BW freezes good faith once present, whereas § 937(2) loses it on later discovery; art. 3:23 BW — knowledge of the public registers excludes good faith.

3. **Period** — varies widely (see the grid).

**DCFR Book VIII Ch 4.** **VIII.-4:101**: an owner-possessor (VIII.-1:206) acquires after **10 years'** continuous possession in good faith throughout (believes and is reasonably justified in believing to be owner; **presumed**), or **30 years** regardless; **never the thief**. **VIII.-4:102** cultural objects (30/50). **VIII.-4:103** continuity (involuntary loss does not break it if recovered or sued for within a year). Period rules: **4:201** incapacity; **4:202** impediment beyond the owner's control in the last six months (not knowing where the goods are is not enough); **4:203** judicial proceedings; **4:204** negotiations; **4:205** acknowledgement ends the period; **4:206** predecessor's time counts if requirements cumulatively met. Effects: **4:301** original owner loses, possessor acquires; limited rights known to the possessor survive; **4:302** original owner loses unjustified-enrichment and tort claims. **Immovables are excluded** from Book VIII (I.-1:101(2)(f)).

**Prescription and land registers.** Positive registers (DE) resist prescription except to cure the register (§ 900 BGB *Buchersitzung*: 30 years registered possession; § 902 registered claims not time-barred); negative registers (NL) tolerate it — prescription need not be registered (art. 3:24(2) BW), though it is a registrable fact (art. 3:17(1)(i)).

**National anchors (verified in the national provisions file unless noted).** NL: art. 3:99 BW (3 years good-faith possession for movables; 10 for other property); art. 3:105(1) (possessor at expiry of the owner's action acquires even in bad faith — the extinctive→acquisitive link); art. 3:306 (20-year general limitation); art. 3:107(1) (possession = holding for oneself — Hoops). DE: § 937 BGB (10 years; bad faith excludes) — Hoops. Others in Hoops: South Africa Prescription Act 1969 s. 1 (30 years); Louisiana CC arts 3473–3491; Italian CC arts 1158–1161.

## Part C — J.A. Pye (Oxford) Ltd v United Kingdom (ECtHR)

**Facts.** Pye companies were registered owners of 23 ha of agricultural land with development potential; the Grahams stayed after a grazing agreement expired in 1983 and used the land without permission until 1999. Under the **Limitation Act 1980** (12-year bar) and **Land Registration Act 1925** (registered owner then holds on trust for the squatter) the House of Lords found title lost — Lord Bingham deciding "with no enthusiasm". The **Land Registration Act 2002** (not retroactive) now requires notice to the registered owner after 10 years and a two-year window to act.

**Procedural history — get the direction right.** Chamber (2005): **violation** of A1P1. **Grand Chamber, 30 August 2007: no violation, ten votes to seven.**

**Grand Chamber reasoning.** A1P1 applicable; the loss characterised as a **control of use** (second paragraph), **not a deprivation**; the limitation period and extinction of title pursue a **legitimate general interest** (legal certainty), within a **wide margin of appreciation**, notwithstanding registration; the **fair balance** was not upset — the rules were long-standing and known, minimal action (demanding rent, suing) would have stopped time, compensation sits uneasily with limitation periods and the deprivation case-law on compensation does not apply to control of use, procedural protection existed, and the value of the land is irrelevant. Related: *James v UK* (1986) (margin of appreciation); *Jahn v Germany* [GC] 2005 (individual and excessive burden).

> PRECISION trap: stating Pye backwards, calling it a deprivation, or assuming compensation was required. The project's Pye file is the ECtHR Information Note on the Grand Chamber only; the Chamber reversal comes from the lecture.

## Exam hooks and traps

- "A grants a usufruct to B, later a mortgage/usufruct to C — rights on default or expiry?" → prior tempore; nemo plus; enforcement effect.

- "Seller under retention of ownership; buyer pledges / manufactures; buyer insolvent" → NL/DE/FR automatic effect vs DCFR registration + 35 days + superpriority.

- "Explain creation vs effectiveness under Book IX and how ranking works."

- "X fences part of the neighbour's land for 12 years — owner?" → control test; good faith; periods; register type.

- "Buyer under a void contract holds a bicycle for 4 years" → healing a failed transfer; art. 3:99 BW vs § 937 BGB vs VIII.-4:101.

- "Is adverse possession compatible with A1P1?" → applicability → interference type → aim → fair balance (Pye).

- **Traps:** treating a limited right as personal; ranking DCFR security rights by creation instead of effectiveness; confusing acquisitive with extinctive prescription; forgetting the DCFR excludes immovables; saying bad-faith acquisition is impossible in NL (possible after 20 years via 3:105).
