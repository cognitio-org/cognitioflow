# Week 2 — Production, Commingling, Combination

*Verstijlen · DCFR Book VIII Ch 5 · van Vliet, ZEuP 2011 · Engine E3*

> Gate: ask original or derivative? These are original modes — a fresh title arises by operation of law, so nemo dat and the transferor's power to dispose are irrelevant. The question is always twofold: who owns the result, and what does the loser get (payment, secured by a proprietary security right — VIII.-5:204).

## Comparative points flagged in the slides

- The **DCFR protects the losing component-owner more than NL does**: payment plus a proprietary security right in the new or combined goods.

- **NL unity principle**: what common opinion regards as a unit is a unit in law; these rules are mandatory, with far less party autonomy than in the law of obligations.

- **German law** distinguishes essential (*wesentliche*) from non-essential components (§ 93 BGB).

- **Production without consent (theft)** is an exception under VIII.-5:201(2).

## Key cases (non-compulsory, discussed in lecture)

- **Teixeira de Mattos** — HR 12 January 1968, ECLI:NL:HR:1968:AC2286 — mixed share certificates; commingling.

- **Hollander's Kuikenbroederij** — HR 24 March 1995, ECLI:NL:HR:1995:ZC1680 — chicken and egg; production/specificatio.

- **Breda/Antonius** — HR 5 October 1990, NJ 1992/226 — who "produces" is decided objectively (decisive influence, risk); art. 5:16 BW is mandatory (van Vliet).

- **Borden v Scottish Timber Products** [1981] Ch 25 — no reservation of property in chipboard made from resin; the new product originates with the manufacturer.

- **Modelboard v Outer Box** [1992] BCC 945 — a clause extending retention of title to products creates a charge, void if unregistered.

- **BGH 3 March 1956 (BGHZ 20, 159); BGH 19 October 1966 (BGHZ 46, 117)** — manufacturing clause (*Verarbeitungsklausel*) makes the supplier the "producer" under § 950; co-ownership clause valid.

## Retention of ownership meets production (van Vliet Part III; Verstijlen)

VIII.-5:201 would give the *producer* ownership and the material owner a secured claim. Under the DCFR the seller under retention of ownership is redirected to Book IX: the buyer is treated as owner for Ch 5 purposes (**IX.-2:308**); an extension of retention to products is possible only by explicit prior agreement, takes the form of a **limited security right** (not ownership), needs **registration** (IX.-3:107) but enjoys acquisition-finance **superpriority** (IX.-4:102, continued via IX.-4:103). Comparative: NL — no extension (art. 5:16 mandatory); England — extension = registrable charge; France — extension allowed but insolvency rules mandatory (*existence en nature* test, L624-16; revindication of resale price); Germany — manufacturing clause honoured by the BGH, heavily criticised.

## Working-group questions (Week 2 sheet — verbatim)

1. Under the rules of the DCFR, can parties regulate the legal consequences of 'production', 'commingling' and 'combination' by contract? Is it possible to regulate the consequences of 'production', 'commingling' and 'combination' in Dutch law?

2. Fruity is a producer of apple juice on the countryside of Groningen. In October, farmers from around the city of Groningen bring their harvest to Fruity for the production of its famous apple juice.
   a. Fruity steals 5 tons of apples from farmer Jo. The same evening Fruity uses the apples to make apple juice. Under the rules of the DCFR, who is the owner of the apple juice, and on which legal ground?
   b. Please assume that Fruity not only steals 5 tons of apples from farmer Jo, but also 5 tons of apples from farmer Ben. Out of the 10 tons of apples, Fruity makes delicious apple juice. Under the rules of the DCFR, who is the owner of the apple juice, and on which legal ground?
   c. What would be the answer of the b-question under Dutch Law?

3. Mike is the owner of a car, a Mercedes old timer. Mike lends the car to James for a week. A few days later, James uses the car to make a trip to his parents. When he arrives at his parents' place, it appears that the rear window of the car is broken. James' father loves repairing old cars, and he replaces the broken window of Mike's car with a spare window of his own, worth € 20. At the end of the week, James returns the car to Mike and he asks Mike to pay € 20 so he can give it to his father. Mike refuses to pay for the new window. James' father therefore would like to get the window back, and he sends Mike an e-mail in which he explains that he is the owner of the window. Mike doesn't agree; he argues that he is the owner of the window now. They turn to you for legal advice. Can you explain who is right?
   a. Please assume that separation of the window from the car would cost € 250. Assume further that the rules of the DCFR apply in this case.
   b. Would the answer be different if the window could be easily removed so that the costs of separation would be trifling (close to zero)?
   c. What would the answer of the b-question be under Dutch Law?

4. Anton manufactures custom-made computers. Anton is working on a computer for a famous architect. The computer is almost finished on 1 November. At that moment the computer has an estimated market value of € 9000. The only thing that is missing is an advanced video card. Anton does not have such a card at the moment and he decides to use the video card of Thomas without his consent (value: € 1000). He assembles the video card into the computer which took him four hours (which is a normal amount of time for this type of computer). The architect is willing to pay € 12.500 for the computer. Anton intends to sell and deliver the computer for that price to the architect.
   a. Please assume that the DCFR is applicable. What is the proprietary position of Thomas in respect of the computer and the video card?
   b. Would the answer be different if Anton has used the video card of Thomas as a result of an honest mistake?

5. Thom and William each own 50 sheep. Each sheep has a microchip which allows the sheep to be identified at the end of the summer grazing period. During the summer, the 100 sheep are held on the same grazing land by a herdsman: Anton. At the end of the grazing period, it turns out that the herdsman – Anton – can't tell which sheep belongs to whom as he is not aware of the microchips. Under the rules of the DCFR, who is the owner of the sheep now?

## Exam hooks and traps

- **Q1 shape:** party autonomy — DCFR yes (VIII.-5:101), NL no (mandatory unity principle).

- **Q2 shape:** production; the theft exception in VIII.-5:201(2) → ownership stays with the material owners via 5:202 (co-ownership by value); the labour contributor is left with an unjustified-enrichment claim.

- **Q3 shape:** combination; the separation-cost threshold in VIII.-5:203(1) ("impossible or economically unreasonable"); trifling cost → no combination, the window remains a separate object.

- **Q4 shape:** principal part (computer €9,000 vs card €1,000); Thomas loses ownership but gets payment secured by a proprietary security right (VIII.-5:203(2), 5:204); bad faith without consent → co-ownership unless the principal part is much more valuable.

- **Q6 shape:** are the sheep commingled at all? They remain identifiable via microchips — arguably no commingling; if treated as commingled, co-ownership by value (VIII.-5:202).

- **Traps:** mislabelling combination as commingling; forgetting the compensation/security-right leg; running nemo dat on an original mode; NL mandatory vs DCFR dispositive.
