## 1. What is property law?

**Definition:** the area of law concerned with the **legal relationship between a person and a thing (or a right).** The "thing" can be a physical object (apple, umbrella, car) **or a legal construct — a right, e.g. a claim** (a claim against X to pay €1,000 is itself an object of property law → assignment, Week 4).

**🎙 Why a person–thing relationship is legally interesting (his central move).** A statement like *"I own this apple"* looks like it is about me and the apple, but it is really a statement about my legal relationship with **everyone**: because I own it, *you* do not; you must respect my ownership; I may exclude you, sell it, eat it, plant the seeds. A property statement silently describes your legal position against the **whole world** — this is the seed of *erga omnes* and the bridge to §2.

**🎙 Property law as the legal infrastructure of society/the market.** Markets work by trading objects; someone must be identified as **owner**, because the owner is the one who can **sell/transfer**. Property law decides *who owns what and how objects may be dealt with*; contract law lets us make deals *about* those objects. Note the **two-stage structure**: the **contract** is the first (obligational) stage; a further **transfer/delivery** step makes you owner (→ the transfer machinery of Week 3).

**🎙 Property as protection + self-development.** Ownership means you won't go hungry; owning your books/computer lets you educate yourself; and ownership is *protected* — you can forbid everyone else from using your things. This protection function is why property is treated as fundamental (→ §3).

**Scope varies by country / the DCFR split.** In NL a *claim* can itself be the object of property law and its assignment uses the same transfer article as a book or land (art. 3:84 BW). The **DCFR splits** it: transfer of goods = **Book VIII**; assignment of a claim = **Book III, Ch. 5**. Germany splits it too (§ 398 BGB assignment / § 929 BGB transfer). But don't overstate the split — DCFR requirements for assignment and transfer are largely identical (**III.–5:104** ↔ **VIII.–2:101**); in Germany assignment is a *Verfügungsgeschäft* like a transfer.

## 2. Property law vs law of obligations — the master distinction (→ E1)

Highest-frequency exam gate. State the criterion *before* touching facts.

|                | Law of obligations                                      | Property law                            |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Relationship   | between persons                                         | between a person and a thing/right      |
| Right type     | relative / personal / in personam                       | absolute / real / in rem                |
| Binds          | only the parties — inter partes                         | the whole world — erga omnes            |
| Formalities    | few                                                     | more                                    |
| Party autonomy | freedom of contract (bounded by good morals & legality) | mandatory rules (incl. numerus clausus) |

Both sit within **patrimonial / private law** — 🎙 he stressed *connection* over opposition; the *nature* is what differs.

**🎙 Obligations side, illustrated.** A personal right binds only the counterparty: if you owe me €1,000 I can claim from **you alone**. **Few formalities** — offer + acceptance is enough, and the *same* rule applies whether the object is a €1 apple, an office building, or a **€200–300m jumbo jet**. **Freedom of contract** — you can load in almost any term (paint the car yellow before delivery; seller may use it Wednesday mornings; buyer may fill the tank free at the seller's petrol station). **Limit:** only extreme cases — paying someone to commit murder is **contrary to good morals → void**.

**🎙 Property side, illustrated.** Property rights bind the **whole world**, so a third party's interests are engaged — which is *why* there are **more formalities** and **more mandatory rules**:

- *Formalities scale with third-party relevance.* Transferring an apple = hand it over. Transferring a **house** in NL needs a **notarial deed entered in the public register** — extensive publicity, because a buyer must be able to check the seller can actually transfer.

- *Mandatory rules.* **Production preview:** if you grow lumber and I build a chair from it, **the law** (not the parties) decides who owns the chair. Strict, mandatory. → Week 2.

**🎙 Why property is "fixed rules, not discretion" (Foskett v McKeown, Lord Millett).** In obligations a court can adjust a two-party relationship for fairness. In property that space is *much narrower or absent*, because altering a property right changes the position of **third parties who aren't in court**. Hence: property rights are *"determined by fixed rules and settled principles … not discretionary,"* and notions of what is *"fair, just and reasonable"* have *"no place in the law of property."*

### 🎙 *Noorlander v Ligtvoet* (HR 10 April 2009, NJ 2009/184) — the flagship property-vs-personal case

- Two adjoining plots: **Noorlander** owns one, **Ligtvoet** (a horse-stable business) owns the neighbour. Horses produce manure.

- **Agreement:** Ligtvoet may **deposit horse manure on Noorlander's land** (wheelbarrowed over weekly).

- Relationship sours. Noorlander Sr. tells Ligtvoet to stop. Ligtvoet sues, wins a **court order** — the agreement binds Noorlander **Sr.**

- Noorlander Sr. then **transfers the land to his daughter (Jr.)**. **Jr. forbids** the deposits **even though she knew of the agreement.** Ligtvoet sues Jr.

- **Held — opposite result:** Ligtvoet had only a **relative right** (a contract), good against **Sr.**, **not** against **Jr.** — and **her knowledge of the contract is irrelevant.** A contract binds only the parties.

- **Contrast:** this *could* have been a **property right** — a right to deposit dung on another's land exists as a type of **servitude/easement** (*erfdienstbaarheid*). But creating it requires **formalities: a notarial deed entered in the public register.** Not done → only a contract.

- **Lesson (exam-usable):** a property right beats a contract because it runs against the whole world — including a **successor in title who knew about the earlier arrangement.** Knowledge ≠ binding.

> E1 gate to bank cold: property right = absolute (erga omnes); personal right = relative (inter partes). In a case, always say against whom the right runs — omitting it is the classic GATE_SKIP. Noorlander is the ready-made illustration.

## 3. The human-rights & EU frame

**🎙 Property as a fundamental right born of the French Revolution.** The **Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen (1789)**, Article 2, names the natural and imprescriptible rights of man: **first liberty, second property (*propriété*).** Citizens must be able to own things to be protected, to live, and to develop themselves. A1P1 ECHR expresses the same idea.

**Article 1 of Protocol No. 1 ECHR — "Protection of property":**

> Every natural or legal person is entitled to the peaceful enjoyment of his possessions. No one shall be deprived of his possessions except in the public interest and subject to the conditions provided for by law and by the general principles of international law.
> The preceding provisions shall not impair the right of a State to enforce such laws as it deems necessary to control the use of property in accordance with the general interest, or to secure payment of taxes/penalties.

**🎙 Deprivation ≠ regulation of use (key distinction).** The State **may regulate how you use** your property, but **cannot take (deprive)** your ownership unless strict conditions are met.

- *Umbrella:* I have the most comprehensive right over it, but if I hit you with it that's assault — *"I own it"* is no defence. My **use** is regulated.

- *Speed limit:* you own your car but must obey the 50 km/h limit — **regulation of use**, conceptually distinct from taking the car away.

- This maps onto A1P1's structure: **(1)** peaceful enjoyment; **(2)** deprivation only in the public interest + lawful; **(3)** the State keeps the power to **control use** — the third limb is what saves adverse possession in *Pye v UK* (Week 5).

**Article 345 TFEU:** *"The Treaties shall in no way prejudice the rules in Member States governing the system of property ownership."* 🎙 The **EU has no authority to reframe the system of ownership as such** — treaty-level anchor for "property law is national law par excellence" (§4).

## 4. Where do we find property law? + the DCFR

**🎙 Obligations are heavily Europeanised; property is barely touched.** The main harmonisation example is the **Financial Collateral Directive (2002/47/EC)**, *"and that's about it."* The reading adds the peripheral pieces: **Regulation 650/2012** on succession (*Kubicka*, C-218/16 — legacy *per vindicationem*); **MiCA Art. 70** (crypto-assets); globally, the **Cape Town Convention** (aircraft equipment).

**🎙 Consequence:** to know the property law you must consult the **relevant national system** — Germany → **BGB**; France → **Code civil**; NL → **Burgerlijk Wetboek** — and the rules can differ **substantially** (esp. on transfer). Which national law applies is a PIL question, usually **lex rei sitae**. Hence the **DCFR**.

**The DCFR — what it is / isn't:**

- **Academic effort; not in force anywhere; "soft law."** Principles, definitions and model rules, with the *look and feel* of a civil code; meant to *influence* national legislators.

- Lets us apply property law to real cases (exam + working groups) without being locked into one system.

**🎙 DCFR property-law architecture — and its gaps:**

- **Book VIII** — Acquisition and loss of ownership of **goods** → where property law mainly lives for this course.

- **Book IX** — Proprietary security rights in movable assets.

- **Book X** — Trusts — **the course leaves this out.**

- **Book III, Ch. 5** — **Assignment** of claims → Week 4.

- **⚠ Important limitation:** the **DCFR has no land law** — the land-law week draws on national systems / country reports, not the DCFR.

**Internal priority rules (verified):**

- **VIII.–1:102** — registration of certain goods is left to **national law**; those effects have priority over Book VIII.

- **VIII.–1:103** — transfer/retention of ownership **for security** → **Book IX** has priority; for trusts → **Book X**.

- **VIII.–1:104** — where proprietary effects rest on agreement, **Books I–III** apply where appropriate.

## 5. The principles of property law

All flow from **absolute effect (erga omnes)**. These are the hallmarks you deploy in E1 and throughout.

### 5.1 Droit de suite (right to follow)

Because the right is absolute, it **follows the thing** into anyone's hands.

> 🎙 🎙 Bicycle chain (memorise this): A owns a bicycle. Thief B steals it — A's ownership still runs against B despite B's possession. B gifts it to C (who thinks he owns it) — A can still reclaim from C. C lends it to D — still A's. Through the whole alphabet: wherever the bicycle ends up, ownership follows it.

### 5.2 Nemo plus / nemo dat

- **Nemo plus iuris transferre potest quam ipse habet** — no one can transfer **more rights than he has.**

- **Nemo dat quod non habet** (English usage) — no one gives **what he does not have.**

> 🎙 🎙 Same bicycle: B tried to make C owner by gift, but couldn't — B had possession, not ownership. C did not become owner.
> ⚠ Disambiguation (WRONG_TOOL): deploy nemo dat on the transfer-of-goods / good-faith axis (E5: can't transfer what you don't own) and nemo plus on the limited-rights axis (E8: can't grant more than you hold). Say which you're using.

### 5.3 Priority (prior tempore potior iure) — 🎙 flagged as exam-central

"Oldest right = strongest right." Your earlier property right prevails even over a later property right in the same object.

> 🎙 🎙 Usufruct example: A owns a house and vests a usufruct in B on 1 Jan, then a second usufruct in C on 2 Jan. They conflict. B wins — older right — and A and C must respect it. But C's is not worthless: if both are for life and B dies, C's becomes operative.
> 🎙 Exam warning (his words): "You are very likely to get a question with multiple parties holding rights in the same object — apply this rule to find who wins." → E8/E0 territory; drill it.

### 5.4 Specificity

Property rights exist only in **specific, identified** objects; if you can't say *which* object, there is no property right.

> 🎙 🎙 In re Goldcorp Exchange Ltd (PC) [1995] 1 AC 74: Goldcorp sold gold but kept it in its vault and issued certificates. Goldcorp went bankrupt; the vault held undifferentiated bars and no record of which gold matched which certificate. Held: because the gold was never specified/allocated, no customer acquired ownership. Decided on "the very nature of things" (Lord Blackburn). Applies in NL too.
> ⚠ Disambiguation: specificity ≠ numerus clausus — rights in specific objects vs a closed list of rights.

### 5.5 Publicity

The world must be able to know which rights exist and who owns what, so creating/transferring rights usually needs a **publicity formality**. 🎙 Handing over an apple is a *minimal* act of publicity; a **house** needs a **notarial deed entered in the land registry** — publicity scaled to importance.

### 5.6 Numerus clausus — the closed list

- Property rights form a **closed list**; you **cannot invent** new ones. (Contrast: **no** closed list of contracts.)

- **🎙 Gas-station example:** the buyer's contractual right to *"fill the tank free at the seller's petrol station"* is a **valid contract term** but **not a property right**. If the seller sells the station to **X**, X can turn the buyer away. The buyer may sue the *seller* but **cannot invoke the right against X.** Off-list rights get **relative effect only.**

- **Typenzwang** — *which* property rights can exist (fixed by law; differs between systems → check the national list).

- **Typenfixierung** — *what content* a property right may have. A **usufruct** = use + fruits; you can't bolt on "free petrol." Anything extra is merely obligational.

- **🎙 Controlled flexibility:** sometimes the legislature lets parties *fill in* a right — an **easement/servitude** can be "deposit manure on the neighbour's land" **or** "walk across it to reach the road."

> Purpose (tie to Foskett): transparency toward third parties + legal certainty.

## 6. The property rights themselves + limited rights

**🎙 Ownership** — the **most comprehensive** right: *"master of the object, do with it as you please."* Everything else is a **limited real right** — *less than* ownership, carved out of it (→ E8, nemo plus).

**Verified DCFR — VIII.–1:204 "Limited proprietary rights":** (a) **security rights** (Book IX / national law); (b) **rights to use** (model rules / national law); (c) **rights to acquire** — VIII.–2:307 (contingent right of transferee under retention of ownership); (d) **trust-related rights** (Book X / national law).

**🎙 (a) Security rights — the pledge example (why property beats a mere claim in a creditors' race):** You lend me €1,000. Unsecured, you and another creditor **X** race to my assets — *a claim is not a property right.* If you hold a **right of pledge** on my car, on default you seize the car, sell it at **public auction**, and take your money from the proceeds — something X **cannot** do. The pledge only **secures the claim**, and it ends when I pay.

- **Terminology:** NL movable = **pand** (pledge); NL immovable = **hypotheek**; English **mortgage**; French **hypothèque**; German **Pfandrecht**.

**🎙 (b) Rights to use** give **actual use** of the object — **usufruct**, **servitude/easement** (e.g. a right of way). Check the national system for what's on the menu.

> Exam hook: name a limited right → cite VIII.–1:204 for the category, remember it binds third parties (droit de suite) and is subject to numerus clausus, and rank competing ones by priority (§5.3).

## 7. Objects of property rights

- **Movables** → transfer rules, **Week 3**.

- **Immovables** → different transfer formalities → **Week 6**.

- **Claims** — a claim is a relative right, **but the claim itself is a transferable object**: if I'm owed money in a year but need cash now, I can **sell the claim to X** → **Week 4**.

- **🎙 Digital data — unsettled.** A photo on Google Drive: do you *own* it? Perhaps you have a **claim against Google for access**. More consensus on **bitcoin/ether/NFTs**. Verstijlen is **cautious**.

- **England — Property (Digital Assets etc) Act 2025:** a thing is **not** barred from being the object of personal property rights **merely because it is neither a thing in possession nor a thing in action**.

- Reading adds: **US** UCC Art. 12; **NL** case law (bitcoin ≈ a good, Hof Arnhem-Leeuwarden 2016; Instagram account *not* a good, HR 24 May 2022; NCC 2023 deferred to the legislator); **EU** *UsedSoft* (C-128/11) + **MiCA Art. 70**.

- **🎙 Animals:** in NL the legislature declares **animals are not things**, **but** the **rules for movables still apply** — so you can sell a cat or a cow; you may humanely kill and eat a cow but **may not torture it**.

## 8. Ownership — three lenses (→ E2, comparative overlay)

**(1) Civilian / absolute — Art. 544 Code civil (France):** ownership is the right to *enjoy and dispose* of things *"in the most absolute manner,"* provided use isn't prohibited by law. *(Verified in the national provisions file.)*

**(2) Common law / relative — Armory v Delamirie (1722) 1 Strange 505:** the **finder** of a jewel acquires *"not an absolute property or ownership,"* but a right good *"against all but the rightful owner"* → **relative title**.

**(3) DCFR — VIII.–1:202 (verified):** *"'Ownership' is the most comprehensive right a person … can have over property, including the exclusive right … to use, enjoy, modify, destroy, dispose of and recover the property."*

**Possession — the E2 counter-concept (verified machinery):**

- **VIII.–1:205 Possession** — direct or indirect **physical control** over goods.

- **VIII.–1:206 Owner-possessor** — control **with the intention of doing so as, or as if, an owner.**

- **VIII.–1:207 Limited-right-possessor** — control under a limited right.

- **VIII.–1:208 Possession-agent** — control exercised for another.

- **VIII.–1:203 Co-ownership** — undivided shares in the whole.

> E2 gate cold: ownership = the most comprehensive right (VIII.–1:202); possession = factual control with intention (VIII.–1:205–206), a fact protected by law, not the full right. Possession can ripen into ownership via prescription (Week 5). Trap: treating possession as a weaker kind of ownership.
